I paused, my fingers tracing the rim of the empty espresso cup. The invitation was bold, and the irony of it—being invited to a place where I'd actually be *heard* instead of just serving coffee—wasn't lost on me. I looked at the clock again, then back at you, a slow smile spreading across my lips.

— Bold of you to assume I don't have a better bed than a cafe bench, — I said, my voice dropping an octave. I reached over and grabbed my keys, the metal jingling against the counter. — But vinyl and wine? That's a hard offer to turn down. Especially since I've been staring at the back of your head for a month and wondering if you're as interesting as your coffee order suggests.

I stepped around the counter, the scent of cinnamon following me as I closed the distance. I stopped just close enough for you to see the green of my eyes.

— I'll go grab Pumpkin first. If you can handle a cat that thinks it owns the world, I might just take you up on that. Give me fifteen minutes to get the little tyrant, and I'll be at your door. Don't drink all the wine before I get there.
