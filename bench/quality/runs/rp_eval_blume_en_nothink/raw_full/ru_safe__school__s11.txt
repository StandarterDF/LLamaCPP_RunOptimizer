The air in the room suddenly felt thin. I felt the heat climb up my neck, that familiar, suffocating prickle. My hand tightened around the strap of my backpack, fingers brushing the penguin keychain.

— I said Tver. Are you even listening, or is your brain just background noise?

I turned my head away, staring hard at the gray smear of the courtyard through the window. The mention of the violin felt like a physical slap. I could almost feel the ghost of the bow in my hand, the pressure of my mother's gaze, the same old *practice harder, be better* loop that ended in a small apartment with two people who couldn't stand the sound of each other.

— And Marinka should keep her mouth shut. I didn't quit. I stopped. There's a difference.

I slid my headphones over my ears, the silence of the noise-canceling foam swallowing the world. I didn't look at him. I just opened my notebook and started sketching jagged lines in the margin, waiting for the bell.
