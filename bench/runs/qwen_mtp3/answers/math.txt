1.  **Analyze the Request:**
    *   **Problem:** A car travels the first half of a journey at 60 km/h and the second half at 40 km/h. Find the average speed for the entire journey.
    *   **Requirements:** Solve step-by-step, explain why the answer isn't 50 km/h (the arithmetic mean).
    *   **Language:** Russian.

2.  **Initial Intuition & Common Pitfall:**
    *   Many people instinctively think: $(60 + 40) / 2 = 50$ km/h.
    *   *Why is this wrong?* Average speed is defined as *Total Distance* divided by *Total Time*. The arithmetic mean of speeds only works if the *time* spent at each speed is equal. Here, the *distance* is split equally, meaning the time spent at 60 km/h is less than the time spent at 40 km/h. Therefore, the average speed must be closer to 40 km/h than to 60 km/h.

3.  **Step-by-Step Solution Plan:**
    *   **Step 1: Define variables.** Let the total distance be $S$. Then each half is $S/2$.
    *   **Step 2: Calculate time for each part.** Use $t = d/v$.
    *   **Step 3: Calculate total time.** Sum the times.
    *   **Step 4: Calculate average speed.** Use $v_{avg} = S_{total} / t_{total}$.
