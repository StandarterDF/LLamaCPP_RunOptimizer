To find the value of $x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3}$ given that $x + \frac{1}{x} = 3$, we can use algebraic identities to derive the answer step by step.

**Step 1: Square the given equation**

We start with the given equation:
$$ x + \frac{1}{x} = 3 $$

Square both sides of the equation:
$$ \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = 3^2 $$

Expand the left side using the identity $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$:
$$ x^2 + 2(x)\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = 9 $$

Simplify the middle term $2(x)\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = 2$:
$$ x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} + 2 = 9 $$

Subtract 2 from both sides to isolate $x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}$:
$$ x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} = 9 - 2 $$
$$ x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} = 7 $$

**Step 2: Cube the original given equation**

We need to find $x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3}$. We can use the identity for the sum of cubes:
$$ a^3 + b^3 = (a+b)(a^2 - ab + b^2) $$

Let $a = x$ and $b = \frac{1}{x}$. Then:
$$ x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3} = \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right) \left(x^2 - x\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^2\right) $$

Simplify the expression inside the second parenthesis:
$$ x^2 - 1 + \frac{1}{x^2} = x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} - 1 $$

So, the equation becomes:
$$ x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3} = \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right) \left(x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} - 1\right) $$

**Step 3: Substitute known values**

From Step 1, we know that $x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} = 7$.
From the original problem statement, we know that $x + \frac{1}{x} = 3$.

Substitute these values into the equation from Step 2:
$$ x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3} = (3) \cdot (7 - 1) $$
$$ x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3} = 3 \cdot 6 $$
$$ x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3} = 18 $$

**Conclusion**

The value of $x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3}$ is 18.

Final Answer: 18
