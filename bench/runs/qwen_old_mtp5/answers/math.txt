    *   **Problem:** A car travels the first half of the distance at 60 km/h and the second half at 40 km/h. Find the average speed for the entire trip.

2.  **Initial Intuition & Common Trap:**
    *   Many people instinctively think the average speed is $(60 + 40) / 2 = 50$ km/h.
    *   *Why is this wrong?* Because average speed is defined as *total distance* divided by *total time*. The time spent at 40 km/h is longer than the time spent at 60 km/h, so the average speed will be weighted towards the slower speed.

    *   **Step 1: Define variables.** Let the total distance be $S$. Then each half is $S/2$.
    *   **Step 2: Calculate time for each part.** Use $t = d/v$.
    *   **Step 3: Calculate total time.** Sum the times.
    *   **Step 4: Calculate average speed.** Use $v_{avg} = S_{total} / t_{total}$.
